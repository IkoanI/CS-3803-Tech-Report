Korean Language Game: Usability Study & Redesign [cite: 1]

Authors: Koan L. Yeng, Jason, Jinho, Eujin, Steve [cite: 2]

Executive Summary

The Korean Language Game was developed to assist students taking introductory Korean courses in mastering basic vocabulary and grammar[cite: 9]. To ensure optimal usability, our team conducted a mixed-methods usability study involving 20 participants[cite: 21, 22]. The study revealed significant user friction, primarily concerning navigation, discoverability, and input handling within the game modules[cite: 62, 133]. Specifically, users experienced frustration with case-sensitive text inputs and the lack of integrated Korean keyboard support during gameplay[cite: 277]. Additionally, the lack of English translations hindered beginner navigation, and accessing flashcards required unnecessary clicks[cite: 277].

To resolve these issues, we developed a high-fidelity prototype that introduces several core enhancements. We implemented a grid layout on the Units page to minimize scrolling[cite: 177]. We added English translations to aid beginners [cite: 178] and integrated accuracy metrics directly on the unit cards so users can quickly evaluate their mastery[cite: 179]. Furthermore, we overhauled the flashcards into a Quizlet-style scrollable design [cite: 272] and replaced the generic feedback box with a structured categorization form[cite: 274]. Ultimately, these design changes aim to streamline the learning process, reduce cognitive load, and foster a more engaging educational environment.

Introduction

The Korean Language Game is a web-based educational platform designed to assist students enrolled in introductory Korean courses (1001/1002)[cite: 9]. Whether users are interacting with the application for formal class requirements or simply to learn basic Korean[cite: 14], the platform aims to provide an engaging alternative to traditional studying methods.

Our target demographic consists of beginner language learners, many of whom have engaged with Korean culture through media such as K-dramas or have plans to travel to Korea[cite: 277]. These users require a highly intuitive interface that supports their learning journey without adding technical cognitive load.

We selected this product for our usability study because while the gamified concept is strong, preliminary observations suggested significant navigation and discoverability issues[cite: 62]. We hypothesized that users would experience input frustration during gameplay [cite: 133] and find the linear layout of the application cumbersome to navigate.

Through empirical testing, we sought to identify exactly where the platform was failing its users. By tracking task completion times and gathering qualitative feedback, we aimed to prove our hypotheses regarding input expectations [cite: 130] and ultimately propose a data-driven redesign to modernize the interface.
